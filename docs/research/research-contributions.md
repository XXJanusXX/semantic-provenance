# Research Contributions

The intended contribution of this research is organised into three areas. Each contribution is bounded by the implementation and empirical evidence actually available.

## 1. Semantic-preservation measurement

A structured semantic-invariant approach for evaluating preservation and loss of task-relevant information across AI-driven transformations.

The evaluation considers semantic structures such as entities, relations, constraints, polarity, temporal information, quantification, modality, policy or authorisation, provenance linkage, event semantics, and quantities or units.

The contribution is an operational measurement and evaluation approach. It is not a claim that the invariant set is universally complete or that the resulting aggregate measure is a universal semantic metric.

## 2. Verifiable transformation provenance

A reproducible technical approach to canonical evidence representation, content addressing, cryptographic integrity and provenance verification across transformation workflows.

The contribution distinguishes:
- Deterministic representation of evidence.
- Content identification and integrity checking.
- Provenance linkage across transformation stages.
- Digital-signature authenticity, where end-to-end signing and verification are demonstrated.

A valid hash, signature or ledger commitment establishes only the cryptographic property actually checked. It does not establish that the underlying semantic content is correct or faithful.

## 3. Reproducible empirical evaluation

A benchmark and experimental methodology for evaluating semantic-preservation and provenance mechanisms, including their limitations, failure modes and operational boundaries.

The contribution includes explicit benchmark definitions, independent ground truth, frozen experimental configurations, case-level accounting, statistical analysis procedures and traceable evidence records.

The value of this contribution includes reproducible negative, null and inconclusive findings, not only positive results.

## Evidence threshold

A contribution should be described as empirically demonstrated only when the relevant implementation, benchmark, ground truth, execution records and analysis are available for review. Design documents, source code, unit tests and successful CI runs are supporting artifacts; none alone establishes a scientific claim.
